One-line summary: A fictional restaurant group treats bilingual inventory labels as an operations project, not just a translation task.

Key points:
- The team locks one glossary for item names, stock states, and refill urgency before publishing any language update.
- Training cards and the live inventory board are updated together so staff members do not learn two different terms for the same action.
- The handoff flow becomes easier to audit because every shift now reads the same labels in the same order.

Why it matters:
Exact language matching is easier to test when the article body is meaningfully localized rather than copied line for line. This variant gives a future multilingual path a realistic but lightweight fixture.

Background:
The fictional kitchens used to rely on informal translations from senior staff. That made new hires dependent on whoever happened to be on shift and often produced different interpretations of the same refill state.

Risks:
If one language is updated later than the other, the bilingual panel can become less reliable than the original single-language version.
